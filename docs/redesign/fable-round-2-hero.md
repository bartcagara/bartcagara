## 5. Answering Bart's own view of the headline (21 Nov)

Bart's view arrived while this round was being written: the headline offers a promise, it is relevant, emotional and specific without the "lose 20 lbs. in 90 days" kind of claim, it is simple, it haunts a man the first time he sees it, and it is short because short communicates confidence. He said I may tell him he is wrong.

**Where I agree, and the masters do too**

- The headline should be a promise. Caples says the same: self-interest first. This is the main reason the control loses. "The founder's last restart." is a name. It promises nothing until the subhead.
- Simple and short read as confidence. Bart ruled the same way on his own profile on 7 Oct, and nothing in the lens argues against it.
- No "20 lbs in 90 days". This reader has heard every fitness claim, and a bigger claim only makes him doubt more (Bencivenga).

**Where the masters push back**

- **Promise or a thought he already has.** Collier and Schwartz want the first line to be something he already says to himself. Candidates C and E do that best and still lost, because they stop at his problem. The answer is a promise said in his thought. "One last time" is that: a promise, and also what a man who has tried too often says to himself.
- **"Specific."** Hopkins would ask for a number in the headline. Bart is right to refuse the cheap one. The honest reading is that this headline is specific to the man, not specific in fact. The facts sit one line down, in "a busy month" and in Drew's "2 months since the 6 month coaching ended".
- **"Haunts."** This is the one place I think Bart asks the headline for too much. No six-word promise will haunt him by itself, and a line built to haunt turns into a poster (slop tell 2). In the lens the thing he still has tomorrow is a real thing: a client's sentence, a number, a message. So the headline's job is to be the promise he can't argue with, and the proof beside it is what follows him home. "One last time" does carry some weight this way: it quietly says he doesn't have many tries left. That is as far as a headline should go.

**The winner against his eight words**

- Promise: yes. It is the benefit, with a verb.
- Relevant: yes. It is the sentence that brought him to the page, and the label names him.
- Emotional: yes, through "one last time". It speaks to the man who is tired of trying. Not through any adjective.
- Specific: partly. See above. This is its softest point.
- Simple: yes. Six words, and Bart already says them out loud.
- Haunts: partly from the headline, mostly from the proof beside it. The nine-months message would do this better than Drew's sentence.
- Confident: yes. It states the promise and stops.
- Not overwritten: the headline, yes. The first screen as a whole is at the limit. By Bart's own standard, the line under the button is the first thing to cut back to "45 minutes with me." if the screen feels crowded, and I would accept that trade.

**Does his view change the pick?** No. It strengthens it. Of the six, B is the only headline that is a promise, in his own words, in six words. The control fails his first criterion.
